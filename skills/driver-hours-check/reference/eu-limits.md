# EU drivers' hours, for reading CO3's figures

Regulation (EC) 561/2006, summarised to read CO3's figures. This is not legal advice; national
rules and derogations differ.

CO3's drive time tool reports, per vehicle record, the remaining time and its limit:

| CO3 capacity | EU limit it follows |
| --- | --- |
| until the next break | 4 h 30 min continuous driving, then a 45 min break (or 15 + 30) |
| today | 9 h daily driving; 10 h at most twice a week |
| this week | 56 h weekly driving; 90 h over two consecutive weeks |

The tool gives the limits as CO3 applied them. Quote CO3's `limitMinutes`; use this table
only to explain them. Rest periods (11 h daily, 45 h weekly, reductions) and the working-time
limits are not in CO3's portal figures.

## The binding figure is usually the next break

A driver with 6 hours left today may be 20 minutes from a mandatory break. Lead with the
capacity that runs out first; the tool names it in `runsOutFirst`.

## Why an estimate can be too generous

When the source is "estimated from driving", CO3 inferred driving from the vehicle's movement.
It cannot see:
- time worked on another vehicle;
- manual tachograph entries;
- whether a stop was a qualifying break or a delivery.

Each of these makes the real remaining time shorter. So an estimate under 30 minutes is inside
its own error: say the tachograph must be checked.
